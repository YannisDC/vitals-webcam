Methods & references — the research behind this app
  • Pulse rate (rPPG): de Haan & Jeanne, “Robust Pulse Rate From Chrominance-Based rPPG”, IEEE TBME 60(3), 2013 (CHROM). Wang, den Brinker, Stuijk, de Haan, “Algorithmic Principles of Remote PPG”, IEEE TBME 64(12):1479, 2017 (POS). Liu et al., “rPPG-Toolbox: Deep Remote PPG Toolbox”, NeurIPS 2023 — benchmark of deep methods (PhysNet, TS-CAN, PhysFormer, RhythmFormer, PhysMamba…).
  • Robustness studies: “The reliability of rPPG under low illumination and elevated heart rates”, npj Digital Medicine, 2025; Nowara et al., meta-analysis of skin-tone fairness in rPPG, CVPRW 2020; iBVP RGB-thermal dataset with signal-quality labels, Electronics 13, 2024; MMPD multi-domain dataset, 2023.
  • Respiratory rate: “Remote Respiration Measurement with RGB Cameras: A Review and Benchmark”, ACM Computing Surveys, 2025 (motion signals most robust); “Seconds Matter: Rapid Non-Contact Monitoring of Heart and Respiratory Rate from Face Videos”, Sensors 26(5):1506, 2026 (dual-modality: face rPPG + torso motion); “Facial Video-Based Robust Measurement of Respiratory Rates in Various Environmental Conditions”, Journal of Sensors, 2023.
  • Blood pressure: Takahashi, Ogawa-Ochiai, Tsumura, “Non-contact method of blood pressure estimation using only facial video” (Windkessel pulse-model fit), 2019; Schrumpf et al., “Assessment of Deep Learning Based Blood Pressure Prediction from PPG and rPPG Signals”, CVPRW 2021; van Putten & Bamford, “Improving Systolic Blood Pressure Prediction from Remote Photoplethysmography Using a Stacked Ensemble Regressor”, CVPRW 2023; Mukkamala, Stergiou, Avolio, “Cuffless Blood Pressure Measurement”, Annual Review of Biomedical Engineering 24, 2022 (calibration still required); “Estimating Blood Pressure with a Camera: An Exploratory Study of Ambulatory Patients with Cardiovascular Disease”, arXiv:2503.00890, 2025; U-FaceBP, arXiv:2412.10679, 2024.
  • Face tracking: Kartynnik et al., “Real-time Facial Surface Geometry from Monocular Video on Mobile GPUs”, arXiv:1907.06724 (MediaPipe FaceMesh / Face Landmarker).

Cuff calibration

Take a reading with a real, validated cuff (upper-arm cuff, seated, 5 min rest), then enter it. Camera BP estimates are anchored to this baseline and track relative deviations.

Deep rPPG model (optional)

Classical POS/CHROM runs always. For extra motion robustness you can load a TS-CAN or EfficientPhys model exported to ONNX ([1,32,3,9,9] diff-frame input — see README). The model runs locally via WebGPU/WASM; estimates are fused with the classical signal.

No deep model loaded — classical POS/CHROM only.
